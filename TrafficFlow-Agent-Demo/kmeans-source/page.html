    Model, dataset & project implementation

    A real K-Means workflow

    1. Validate inputs and standardize four numerical features.
    2. Train K-Means++ with K = 4, five initializations and up to 100 iterations each.
    3. Assign each input to the nearest learned cluster.
    4. Order cluster centers by congestion intensity and attach the four level names.
    5. Estimate a future scenario from local time patterns, then classify it.

    Silhouette measures cluster separation from a sample of up to 160 observations. It is not prediction accuracy. Congestion names are relative to this training dataset.

    Train with your own traffic data

    The built-in dataset contains 2,016 simulated observations across three fictional roads and seven days. No sensor feed or real-world accuracy claim is included.

    40–5,000 rows, up to 2 MB. Required columns: location, time, vehicles, speed, density, occupancy. Time: HH:mm. Use the same units as the inputs. Files stay in this browser tab.

    How the time estimate works

    The app averages nearby historical time slots (within 30 minutes) for the selected location. It adds the change between the current and future averages to your input, then applies K-Means again. At least two observations are needed in both windows. This is an illustrative time-pattern estimate, not a validated forecasting model.
